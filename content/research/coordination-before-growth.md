## Growth is not neutral

Growth expands the number of actors with claims on a system. More capital, more users, more integrations, more governance delegates, and more market surfaces all increase the coordination burden.

When a protocol grows before it can coordinate, every success metric becomes a source of pressure. Liquidity must be defended, token holders must be answered, infrastructure must absorb load, and governance must react without becoming the bottleneck.

## Coordination capacity

Coordination capacity is the ability of a system to make coherent adjustments under pressure. It is not only governance turnout or contributor headcount. It includes treasury policy, risk monitoring, parameter authority, communication discipline, and the ability to distinguish noise from structural change.

## Design implication

The healthier sequence is coordination first, then growth. Build the constraints, escalation paths, and balance sheet logic before the system is forced to discover them during a market event.
